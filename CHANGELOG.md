# Changelog

What changed for the player, in plain words, one section per version (newest first). The technical record is
`docs/HISTORY.md`; the list of versions and their commits is `docs/VERSIONS.md`.

## Unreleased

- **A much taller ladder.** Every level is stretched. An ordinary grown person now stands about 35 (most between 20 and
  50), a soldier 80 to 180, a knight 230 to 299 and more than an ordinary knight past that, a kingdom's elite 300 to 420,
  and only a handful of the continent's strongest between 500 and 700. A grown dragon is about 700, a great demon about
  650, a lesser demon 300 to 500. The legends stand from 800 up, and Sarnavoth, King of Dragons, at 1000. The fights
  between them are as they were: Sarnavoth still a little behind the three Primordial Archdemons together, the Sword Saint
  (now about 875) still even with any one of them and well above the Lord of the Sea. Your own strength, and every beast's,
  is unchanged: only the numbers that read it moved.
- **Calamities.** Twenty-nine new kinds of monster stand between the dragons and the legends: titans, hydras, lich-kings,
  hive-empresses that eat a province bare, tempest rocs, the fire under the mountain, the Grey Hunt, gate-dukes of the
  demons, colossi of the first forge and the serpents that girdle the sea. No one meets them yet: they wait for the map.
- **No more rumours about titles.** Nothing tells you about a title before you earn it: the tavern tales are gone, and so
  is "Things you have heard" in Records. Another person's title shows its name and what it gives, never what he did for
  it. A title is announced when it comes.
- **Beasts where they belong.** A bear, a tiger or a mammoth is an animal again: a soldier's death, but a band's kill, and
  even the kings of the beasts (the Boar-King, the Mountain Father, the Queen of the Canopy) are a fight for knights, not
  a rival to dragons. Trolls and ogres stand from a soldier's match to an elite's. Between 600 and 800 there are now only
  true calamities: grown and elder dragons, great and ancient demons, titans, liches, leviathans, the great spirits and
  the colossi of legend. There are only three ancient dragons under the King (Ash, Frost and Storm), few elder dragons
  and no more than a hundred dragons in the world; thirty-three ancient demons, as strong as the elder dragons, rule
  under the three Archdemons. The dragons of the High Roost are gone.

## v2.1.0-alpha.19 (2026-11-26 10:12 ET) · Skills evolve at level 10, two can merge, a rare leap once a life; titles earned by hidden deeds, stacked under the same limits

- **Titles.** The world now gives names for what you do: the Undefeated, the Unbought, Ironhide, the Hundred-Day Wall,
  the Peacemaker, Beast-Bane, Wyrm-Slayer, the Sword of your realm and more. A title is never bought or trained, and
  what earns one is never shown, nor hinted at.
  Titles stack: each brings honour and renown once, and while you hold it a gift in words (a steadier nerve, a harder
  guard, the regard of soldiers, a little more strength), never past the limits skills already have. Some are one of a
  kind, held by one living person at a time, and some can be lost (a single defeat ends the Undefeated). Your profile
  shows the title you bear beside your name and a row of your titles (press one for its story); Records lists them
  and the ones you lost. Other people's profiles show theirs. The legends keep their titles
  and their strength exactly as before; the Sword Saint's is both a title and a class. Saved games load with no titles.
- **Skills evolve.** When a skill reaches level 10 and you have what its next rank asks (the concepts and, from rank B,
  a deed; thirty years for the highest), it evolves: the new skill begins beside the old one, which you keep. The log
  says why. From rank B, and for your first, a card asks what you make of it: a point for the body, the first months'
  work, renown, or rest.
- **Two skills can become one.** Some pairs at level 10 (sword and shield, command and tactics, trade and stewardship,
  and others) can be joined into a stronger art when someone who knows it is near; a few come only in the worst of
  moments. Joined, both read through the new art and neither grows alone again.
- **A rare leap.** Once in a life, in a fight won far above you or at the edge of death, a skill may leap to the next
  rank at once. It always leaves a mark.
- **Arts: actives, passives and buffs.** Beside your skills you now learn arts of three kinds. Actives are moves you use
  in a fight (Quick Cut, Haft Sweep, Snap Shot, Shield Bash, Hip Throw, Lance Charge and more), each once a fight or
  every few exchanges, for a little or much of your wind. Passives fire by themselves when their moment comes: when you
  are wounded, at the very edge, in the first exchange, against someone stronger, at first light or in the dark. Buffs
  heal or steel you: bind a wound mid-fight, roar a war cry for three exchanges, dress a wound or nurse a fever once a
  month, sharpen your eyes for a season's hunting. You learn them by practice, from a teacher, by what you go through,
  or out of an art you have mastered; they grow as you use them. Ready a few in the places you have (more as you grow
  and master things) on the Skills sheet; what you ready is in hand from the next month. In a fight your readied arts
  are buttons; your foes use theirs too, and some monsters breathe fire or freeze their hides with mana that no person
  has. Every art says what it does in words when you press its name. Saved games load with no arts; what your skills
  already open comes at the first month.

## v2.1.0-alpha.18 (2026-11-26 06:13 ET) · Skills that do things (effects and their caps, step 6) and a person's level on the ladder, earned by defeating people and beasts, with stat points to place

- **Your level is earned.** You now carry a level on the same scale as every beast and legend (an ordinary grown
  person about 25, a soldier about 100). It grows while you grow up; after that only by beating people and beasts in a
  fight, more for a stronger foe and almost nothing for a far weaker one. Each level brings a point to place in strength,
  speed, endurance, reflexes, wits or will: the Profile shows your level and, from age 12, a button to place them
  (points placed stay placed). As a child your household's way of life places them for you; other people place theirs
  by their trade and temper. Saved games load with every stat as it was.

## v2.1.0-alpha.17 (2026-11-26 01:13 ET) · The world grows: the level ladder for every being, 270 species and 16 legends (Sarnavoth, the Primordial Archdemons, the Sword Saint), encounters in the wilds, skills read in rank and level

- **Skills have ranks and levels, not words.** A skill now reads like "Basic Swordsmanship · C · Lv 3": a rank from D
  up to S, and a level from 1 to 10 within it. Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Expert, Master, Grandmaster and Legend
  are gone from every skill row, the skill sheet, the training plan, the "Work at a skill" picker, the choice that asks
  for a skill ("at C · Lv 7, you are D · Lv 6"), the treatise's requirement (now "a skill at rank B") and the log.
- **Each rank's levels are finer.** In ranks C, B and A a level is half of an old step, so your skill shows progress
  sooner and a check reads it as soon as you are half way up. The odds of every check are the same as before on the day
  you load: a saved game opens with every skill reading exactly what it read, and the half step you had already earned
  lands at the end of the first month.
- **A new rank asks a deed, not a proof.** To rise to rank B and above you still need the concepts and one deed of that
  rank (a fight won against one of that rank, a work judged by masters, a battle won, a case carried); the skill sheet
  says "To reach rank B" and what is still wanted.
- Saved games from earlier versions load and carry on.

## v2.1.0-alpha.16 (2026-11-25 17:06 ET) · Phase 1: the Emilio fixes, the exploit stopgaps (breakthroughs, the throne plot), outlaws hunted, one time button
- **The world is far bigger than you.** Every being now has a level, from a cellar rat to the King of Dragons, with ranks
  G to SSS (an ordinary man about 25, a soldier 100, the best of a kingdom 300 to 380, a grown dragon 450, the legends above 600). Titles and mastery count as much as the level; the masters of the giants, dwarves,
  elves and orcs, the great dragons and the old monsters stand far above him. The codex's Power & scale page shows where
  you stand, the great beings you have heard of, ranked, and the beasts you have met.
- **270 kinds of beast and monster**, each with its own ways: what drives it, what provokes it, what turns it away (if
  anything does), how it treats people. Some can be talked to. Monsters in the wild grow into stronger forms, and the
  greatest take a name.
- **The wilds push back.** On the road, exploring, hunting or at the frontier, something may find you: run, try what turns
  its kind, speak to it, or fight. Odds are in words; a fight far above you is over at once. Towns stay mostly safe.
- **Legends move.** Sixteen legends with their own histories, grudges and habits raid, sleep, hunt and bargain for
  reasons the news tells; crowns send hosts against them, and lose. Every child grows up on the story of Cael Vardan, the
  Sword Saint, a hundred and nineteen and still the strongest man alive, keeping watch on his ridge in the Dragon Mountains.

- **Escaping the gallows really frees you.** Breaking free at the gallows or bribing the hangman used to leave you
  "in prison" on paper, so cell fights followed you abroad. Now a man who escapes is out of the cells.
- **No training injuries in a cell.** Your training plan's injuries came even in prison; now only training can hurt you.
- **The story reads in order.** What you did comes first in the year's log, then what it caused ("Your attempt fails."
  then "You are imprisoned"), not the other way round.
- **Murder is not a game you can retry.** One attempt on a person a year; none from a prison cell on someone outside
  it; whoever survives your attempt is on guard against you for years; an outlaw is not "declared outlaw" again.
- **A book takes mastery.** Writing a treatise now asks an Expert's skill and a writer of 28 or more.
- **A refused petition says why** (you asked last year, the crown hardly knows you, or you have done too little).
- **A former superior stops giving you orders** once you have left the post.
- **Breakthroughs are rare.** Beating a far stronger foe can still change you, but at most three times in a life, two
  years apart, and a sparring bout never counts. Your body's limits rise only a little.
- **A throne plot is a once-in-years gamble.** Raising a plot, even one you let cool, closes that road for five years; the
  best odds are lower, and the card says them in words.
- **Outlaws are hunted.** The one you wronged, or the lord of the land, sends men after you when he has reason to.
- **A sacred throne goes to an old churchman,** not a young priest.
- **One time button.** As a child you live a year at a time with **Next year**; from age 12 there is only **Next
  month**.

## v2.1.0-alpha.15 (2026-11-19 09:00 ET) · Everyone ages and the houses go on; the game built by CI; versions cut one way

- **People grow old and die, and the great houses go on.** Before, only rulers, title holders and people you knew ever
  died of their years: a century in, the court was full of men of 150 and more. Now everyone ages and dies by his body
  and his luck, more in a plague year or a famine, and no one outlives his people's span. The families of the world
  marry and have children: a house's heir takes a wife, a couple wants an heir and a spare, so the houses carry on
  instead of dying out. When a grandparent, an uncle or a nephew you never met dies, word reaches you in the story; a
  death in your household goes into the year's summary and does not stop the year.

## v2.1.0-alpha.14 (2026-11-19 05:20 ET) · The realm's peace (stability, factions, coups, private wars, all with reasons); the military path (the muster, units that differ, careers, each weapon's road); a lighter save

- **Officers are paid like officers again.** A soldier still earns a few suns a month, but a captain, a commander and a
  General earn enough to keep their station.
- **The company's drill no longer repeats itself.** A drill your company has done before passes in a line of the story;
  the scene comes for a new drill, a hard month, or one that matters.
- **The chronicle's boasts make sense:** after a victory it never claims fewer enemy dead than our own.

- **Your road through the ranks follows your weapon and your way of fighting.** A lancer rides and learns the sword, then
  commands horse; an archer shoots; a sapper works timber and stone, then plans the works; a scout tracks and keeps
  quiet. The War tab's *Your road* card says what your rung trains, with your gift for each in words ("tracking (gifted)").
- **Each rung has its trial.** When you stand at the edge of a new level, the road sets its own test: the butts at two
  hundred paces, a bridge of boats, a sentry taken without a sound. Standing it is your proof, and the next promotion
  board remembers it.
- **Your way leads to your specialism:** the war bow for a man of the shot, the bill and pike for a man of the wall, the
  lance for a man of the charge.
- **Promotion asks what the next rung needs:** an archer's captaincy the bow and command, a sapper's command tactics and
  craft. Some rungs open a role: a scout's the pathfinder's, the train's the clerk's.

- **Each branch has its own career.** The War tab says what your rank does in your branch, and the whole ladder from
  soldier to General on a tap. Missions follow your branch: scouts count the enemy and take prisoners who talk,
  engineers dig the sap and bridge the river, the train brings the bread up; other work still comes now and then.
- **Promotion weighs what the post needs.** A board asks whether you know what your branch prizes (riding for the
  lances, tracking for the scouts, stores for the train) and, from sergeant up, whether men follow you. The War tab says
  in words what the next board will weigh.
- **Peace service counts.** A duty seen through, and done well, is service the board remembers; long, faithful years at
  one rank raise your chances, and the board says so when it promotes you.
- **Roles have a road.** A good clerk becomes a quartermaster and then master of the train; a surgeon's mate a surgeon.
  Pay rises with each step.
- **Soldiers' pay is a soldier's pay:** a few suns a month for a soldier, more with each command. Wealth in arms comes
  from loot and ransom.
- **Every company in the world has its standard-bearers, surgeons, foragers and drill,** not only yours: armies that
  keep their wounded and their bread fight on longer.

- **No two companies are the same.** Your company has a make-up (veterans or raw men, conscripts, its sergeants, its
  captain's temper), drills it knows well or not at all, and a character that comes from its own history: it broke
  before horse at one fight, held a ford at another, chases too far under a bold captain. The War tab's *Your company*
  card says it all in words.
- **Training fits the unit.** Each season the company's drills are worked out from what it lacks, what is coming (a war,
  enemy horse, a siege, a march), what it must unlearn, and what the ground and its sergeants can teach: no ladders where
  there is no wall, no bridging without a river. As a captain you pick one or two drills a month; below that, your
  captain does, and you are in the drill yard with your company. A drill learned shows on the war map; men driven hard go
  into a fight winded.
- **Scouts, engineers and the train have companies of their own:** the Outriders, the Sappers, the Siege train, the
  Quartermasters.
- **There is always work.** In peace your company has a duty for its reasons: the castle watch, a patrol against
  brigands, the tax escort, the road, the lord's errand, the drill camp. A new soldier has one in his first month.
- **Your role counts in the field.** A standard-bearer, signaller or cook keeps spirits up; a surgeon's mate saves
  wounded men; a forager finds bread; a provost's man keeps men from slipping away; a pathfinder sees further; a
  carpenter gets the division over a river at less cost.
- **You may be asked for.** Once a year an officer of another branch may ask for you, if your skills suit his men better.

- **Joining the army is a muster now, not a click.** Go to your lord's muster or the crown's (whoever holds the land you
  live in), or to a free company's table. The muster-master looks you over and says what he sees: the weapon you know
  and what you carry, how you are built to fight, and anything else of use (a horse, letters, a trade, the sea, tracking,
  medicine, a horn). Then he offers you two or three places, each with his reason ("The bows: you can draw a bow"), and
  says which door is shut ("No horse, so not the lances"). Choose one, or walk home. A man taken by lot gets no offers.
- **Fifteen branches of a feudal army:** the lances, the light horse, the Scout Regiment, the spears, the men-at-arms on
  foot, the bows, the crossbows, the siege engineers, the garrisons, the train, the ships' companies (only where the realm
  has a coast), the household guard and the royal knights, the elite orders, the hired companies and the conscript foot.
  Your branch decides your troop, your arm of service (no more being asked a year later where you belong) and whose
  command you serve in.
- **Roles beside your rank.** A captain who sees you read, dress wounds, shoe a horse or find a road may make you the
  company's clerk, surgeon's mate, farrier or pathfinder (ten roles in all). A role pays a little, teaches its skill, and
  brings its own work.
- **Rank words agree everywhere.** The War tab, the Path card and the chain of command say the same rank and the same men:
  a sergeant a section, a captain a company, a commander a wing, a General a host. The War tab shows your place, your
  branch, what it beats and what beats it, and its work in peace.

- **Smaller saves in a long game.** The story of years more than forty years past keeps what the Chronicle tells of
  them (a decade's weightiest moments, and every great moment and death); the Chronicle itself reads exactly as before.
  A past life's private bookkeeping is let go a year after the death; the person, their family, deeds and renown stay.
  At year 100 a save is about a twentieth smaller, and the story log no longer grows without end. Older saves are
  trimmed the same way when they load.
- **A realm's peace now has reasons you can read.** A realm's stability is no longer a number that wanders by luck: it
  settles toward what holds the realm up and what pulls it down (its old temper, the throne's right, the lords, the
  commons, the towns, the faith, the army, the ruler himself). Press the word (firm, steady, uneasy, shaking, breaking) to
  read them. A great house's loyalty to the crown follows its own reasons too: the crown's laws and taxes, a seat or an
  office, wrongs the crown did it, the head's ambition or honour, another faith, wars lost.
- **Leagues of lords form, petition and rise for reasons.** Two houses or more with the same grievance and a tie between
  them (a border, a liege, kin) make a league under a house great enough to lead it. Its leader decides when to bring
  its demand; the crown grants it, buys them off, arrests the leader or refuses; and the league backs down, waits,
  withholds its taxes and men, or rises. When it rises, every other great lord chooses the crown, the rebels or his own
  hall.
- **Claims, risings and coups have a cause.** A claimant presses or kneels by his blood, the crown's right, the realm's
  state and who would back him. The commons rise only with a leader of their own. A coup needs an opening (a king losing
  his war, ill, a child; a shaking realm), someone who can reach the king, and a motive; the plotters approach others one
  by one, and any of them may tell the crown. If you can reach the king, they may come to you, and the choice is yours.
- **Lords quarrel among themselves.** Two great houses with a boundary, a slight and bad blood between them bring their
  quarrel to the crown; whoever loses the judgment remembers it, and may defy it, raid, and fight it out unless the crown
  enforces its peace. As a ruler, you judge such quarrels. A march lord may raid across a hostile border, and his king
  disowns him, backs him, or lets the march lords fight it out. A great lord set under a General of lower birth may come
  late, or take his men home.
- **A ruler can be made to sue for peace:** by lords who refuse further service, by a council that votes for it where the
  law gives it the vote, or by a coup that puts in a man who will sign. A councillor's counsel of war, an alliance or a
  pact is weighed by the ruler with his own reasons.

## v2.1.0-alpha.13 (2026-11-18 15:25 ET) · Wars end only by surrender or defeat: a ruler's reasons to ask for terms, envoys on the road, terms of real land, coin and crowns

- **Wars end only when one side asks for terms or can fight no more.** There is no hidden war score, no timer and no
  peace nobody asked for. Each month, and after a great battle, each side's ruler weighs his reasons to fight on (his
  cause, his pride, his host, the land he holds, the battles he won, what yielding would cost him) against his reasons to
  ask (his hosts broken, battles lost, his land and capital taken, a worn-out realm, an empty chest, trouble at home, his
  age), bent by his temper: a cautious king asks while he can still bargain, a proud one holds on. A side whose capital
  has fallen with no host left, or whose every host is broken, is beaten and is asked nothing.
- **Envoys ride, and the war goes on while they do.** The asking crown's envoys take days on the road to the other court,
  whose ruler names his terms: what his war was for, or everything he holds, or nothing yet. The asker takes them,
  refuses, or offers the terms of the cause instead. Terms are real things: land his soldiers hold, coin, tribute, a
  public submission, alliances renounced, a claim given up, a claimant crowned, a land set free, and ten years of sworn
  peace. Land, crowns and realms really change hands.
- **As a ruler,** the peace table tells you in words and on a tipped scale how the other crown would answer, and why; no
  percentages. Your envoys ride and bring its answer back. When an enemy's envoys come to ask you for terms, or bring
  terms of their own, or an enemy can fight no more, you decide on a card. A councillor's counsel of peace is weighed by
  the king with his own reasons.
- **The news follows your rank:** a king's council hears why he asks for terms, a commander hears that envoys have ridden,
  everyone in the war hears the peace and its terms. The chronicle says who asked whom for terms, or who could fight no
  more.

## v2.1.0-alpha.12 (2026-11-18 12:45 ET) · Lords with their own armies: the summons and its answers, no levies, conscription only for a named need, desertion and mutiny for reasons

- **No more levies.** A realm's armies are its sworn men (knights, men-at-arms, a lord's retinue, about one in four
  hundred of the people), the regiments its treasury pays, and the roll: volunteers, about one in a hundred, who drill
  some days a year and march when mustered. Nobody is taken by a yearly tax of men.
- **Conscription, only when it is needed.** A crown at war may proclaim a call by lot, and only for a need it can name:
  the enemy on its own soil, a stronghold besieged, a host lost, fewer spears than the enemy. One hearth in twenty for a
  season, one in ten for a year, one in five while the enemy stands on home soil. Each great lord raises it in his own
  lands, in full, by half, or late, for his own reasons; the men drill a month and march as conscript foot in his
  contingent. The fields lose their hands (twice as badly at harvest), and the parishes remember every call: a call for
  their own doors is forgiven, one held past its term or made for a king's pride is not. The law now says how far the
  crown may call on its own word.
- **Men desert and companies mutiny for reasons.** Every company in the field weighs what holds it (an oath, pay on the
  day, their own doors, a captain who wins, years together) against what pulls at it (hunger, pay owed, the harvest at
  home, half the company dead, winter, a term served and still held). Conscripts slip away first; sworn men almost
  never. A mutiny needs a grievance, a man the others follow, and an occasion, and the captain must answer it.
- **If your hearth's lot is drawn,** you go, pay for a substitute, hide in the loft, or take to the roads, each with its
  price. As a ruler you proclaim the call yourself and decide when the conscripts go home. As a soldier, the War tab
  tells you in words how your company holds together.
- **The great lords have their own armies.** Every soldier of a realm now serves in one command: the crown's (the
  king's own regiments, his knights, the orders, hired men, led by Generals he commissions) or a great lord's (a duke,
  or a marquis or count who holds straight of the crown), whose first host the lord leads himself or gives to a General
  of his own. A lord's hosts march together in one army; nobody breaks them up.
- **The summons.** When the crown goes to war, each great lord answers for his own reasons: he comes in full, comes with
  part of his men, comes late, sends a son or a captain, sends shield money, or refuses. The muster roll keeps what
  each said and, underneath, why he really did it: the harvest, a hostile neighbour on his border, his years, an old
  grievance, a faction that wants the king weaker. A lord's men serve the first season at his own cost; after that the
  crown pays them, or he may take them home.
- **A refusal has consequences.** The king decides how far he dares punish a lord who would not come: he may let it
  pass, fine him, take a son to court, take a host from him, call him forsworn before the court, or attaint him. A
  sentence the crown is not strong enough to enforce costs it, and every lord watches. The lord submits, keeps
  silent, or writes to the others who stayed home. A march lord who stayed home still rises when the enemy reaches
  his own lands.
- **How the king commands.** An army of two hosts or more is led by the king in person, by one General given it by the
  king's written word for that war, or, when the king will name nobody, by a council of its Generals, which is slower
  to agree and has no one will. The king chooses for reasons you can read (his skill, his trust in the man, his kin, his
  fear of a General the soldiers love, the great lords' pride).
- **If you are a landed lord,** you are summoned too, with all six answers and what each costs; riding with your men
  takes you into the war, and refusing brings the crown's judgment, which you answer. A General serving in a great
  lord's host answers to that lord. The War tab's muster roll and the news of the muster tell you what your place lets
  you know: the crown hears the whole roll, a lord who stayed home and why, a soldier his own lord's answer and the
  count of banners.

## v2.1.0-alpha.10 (2026-11-18 06:39 ET) · Reasons for everything: no Marshal, minds that decide, skills that grow like people, doors that open by standing, and a map with roads

- **The Dynasty tab explains its figures.** An ending found in an earlier life says how many lives found it, and the
  inheritance card's purse, lands, renown and friends each open what passes to your heir and why.
- **A friend's next rank reads in words even after a quarrel.** When more wrong than kindness lies between you, the
  pips say so instead of showing a number below nothing.
- **Your uncle's mill begins only in a family that has the uncle.** The story no longer starts, and stalls, in a life
  whose tree holds no unwed uncle and no grown cousin.

- **A fighter's odds against you read truer.** The scale on a person's page ("would usually beat you") overstated your
  chances by three to five points in close fights; it now reads what the duels do.
- **Skills with levels and experience; the proficiencies are gone.** Every skill now has a level in words, Beginner to
  Legend, each in five steps ("Swordsmanship: Expert II"), and a bar toward the next step. You earn experience by doing
  things: a month of training with or without a teacher, the work of your post, a card's test, a fight, a lesson. A
  beginner climbs quickly; a master's step takes years. Twenty-eight skills in eleven groups; the old crossbow, lance,
  knife and the rest are specialisms of their skill now.
- **A new word is not bought with practice.** Each word asks one or two named ideas you must grasp ("the measure", "the
  temper"). Some come at once, some take years, and a few never come. Until one does, the bar stays full and the row
  says why: "held: the bind". What you keep working is saved, and lands all at once when the idea comes. From Expert up
  you must also prove it: a fight won against someone of that word, a work judged, a battle commanded.
- **Breakthroughs.** Sometimes an idea comes all at once: beaten by a better hand, a near death, a rival, a new land, a
  master's word, a quiet winter after long work. Each goes into the skill's story.
- **Growth moves with your life:** unbroken months of work, a rival, a teacher who has the idea himself, a new place,
  rest, purpose, too many things at once, a specialism, pride. A skill's sheet (tap its row) says what is in force this
  season, how fast it is coming and why, its ideas and how near each is, its talent letter, knacks and story.
- **Work at a skill** replaces "Practice a proficiency": practise alone, take a lesson, seek a harder test, or think
  it over. Twelve new moments: the master's word, the chance to be judged, the rival, the quiet winter, a specialism,
  "some never see it", and more.
- **The season's line** now says in words what rose and chiefly why, and what held and why.
- **Skills grow at a living pace.** What you do in a month counts more toward a step, ideas come a little sooner in a
  busy life, and once you are ready for a new word the chance to prove it comes to you within a year.
- **Old saves:** every skill becomes the level under its old number (no word is lost; the rest goes on the bar), the
  folded skills become specialisms, and gifts become talent letters.
- **Every relationship needs a base.** A king will not get to know a peasant, and most lords will not look at one. Who
  lets you near follows the gap between you, his pride and his temper, his own birth (a baron risen from the plough
  sympathises; a duke born to gold does not), what he needs of you (your sword in a war, your skill, your silence, your
  coin, your voice in council) and what you share (you serve him, stood in his line, shared his cell or his lessons).
  You shift the scales by knighthood and a good name, mastery, renown, wealth, a deed, and a friend who knows you both.
  What is out of reach is not offered, and a note says why; what is within reach but not sure is an attempt, and he
  answers with his reason ("Lord Aymer will hear you now: a knight of your name is worth an hour"). A refusal stands for
  the year. Patrons, a great house's match for your child and your friends' circles follow the same base.
- **The map has roads, fords and passes.** High roads and tracks join the towns, most of which now stand on a river or the shore;
  every road meets a river at a ford or a bridge, and mountain roads go over named passes. Woods, marsh, tributaries and the
  names of seas, ranges, rivers and forests are on the map, and no two neighbouring realms share a colour.
- **The ground matters.** You travel and armies march faster on the roads, supply runs along roads and rivers, and an attack
  across a river or through a pass costs the attacker, as the battle report says. Oases lie in dry country, and hills no longer
  cover half the land.
- **Kings and lords decide for reasons you can read.** A crown goes to war, a great house joins or leaves a faction, and
  the king names his first host's General by weighing named reasons (an insult, bad blood, a cause, his Generals' count
  of the enemy's spears, an old wrong, a debt, his temper), never by a roll of the dice alone. The news of a war now ends
  with "Because: ... Despite: ...". You hear the reasons anyone could know; the council's own count of spears only if
  you sit on that council or wear that crown.
- **Rulers have feelings.** An envoy shamed, a raid, a murder or a lost war leaves a ruler with wounded pride, anger or
  shame, which cools with time (slowly in a vengeful man). A proud, hot-headed king may declare a war his Generals told
  him he would lose; a cold one keeps the wrong and strikes later, when the odds have turned.
- **Factions say why.** A new faction carries its founder's reasons on the Factions card, and a faction raised in your
  own realm is news.

- **No Marshal; five ranks.** The army has five ranks on every screen: soldier, sergeant, captain, commander and General.
  General is the highest; there is no Marshal and no High General. A sergeant leads a section of about twelve, a captain
  a company of about two hundred, a commander a wing of several companies, and a General a host (a division on the war
  map). A rank with two steps says so: "Captain (second step)".
- **One General to each host.** A realm has as many Generals as it fields hosts; a division worn below three thousand
  men is a column under a commander. An army of several hosts is led by one General given it by the king's written word
  for that war, and the order of battle names him the commanding General.
- **The senior General.** The General of the crown's first host speaks for the army at the king's council, does the
  council's yearly work for the army, and commands no other General. A General may be named to it; a captain may not.
- **Old saves:** a Marshal becomes his realm's senior General, and a player who was Marshal is a General holding the
  first host and the army's place at council. Old rank words in names and styles read as the five ranks.

- **Start a new life works.** After a life was saved, "Start the new life" asked the same question again for ever, and the
  menu stayed open over the creation screen. Both are fixed.
- **Nothing is handed to you.** Ambitions, chapters and their aims are gone, and the top of Life no longer suggests what to
  do: it keeps only what you have taken on (your debts, favours you owe, promises made). The scenes once written for an
  ambition now come to a life that goes looking for the thing: set your plan's aim, choose your training, take a post.
- **A king's life is not cheap.** You must be able to get near him; an attempt can be found out (the guard, his
  spymaster, the servants who saw you before), and it is remembered. The sentence follows the realm's law and the king's
  nature, death for treason more often than not, and the block is real. Begging his pardon depends on what he thinks of
  you, his temper, what you did and what you have given since. A murdered king stays dead: his heir takes the crown, the
  realm is shaken, the court hunts the hand, and once it is known his kin swear vengeance and a price is set on your head.
- **Soldiers are sent out.** Your unit gets work month by month from its war, its post and its officer, and a new man is
  tried within his first months. Each mission says who sent you and why.
- **Next month and Next year finish what they start.** Something that matters stops the step midway; once you have answered
  it (or the battle is over) the month or the year carries on to its end. A wound, an illness, a death or a birth in the
  family is written in the year's summary instead of cutting the year short.

## v2.1.0-alpha.5 (2026-11-14 18:49 ET) · v2.1 foundations: posts, bodies, honest cards, war at your rank

- **Fairer endings.** A long life that was really a saint's, a scholar's or a keeper of secrets' is remembered for that,
  not just its purse: "The Wealthy" now comes after them.
- **Odds that add up.** Every line in the odds popover adds up to the number on the choice, even for a near-sure thing.
- **Scenes on their dates, fixed.** A scene that only mentions a month as a plan ("she means to cross the passes in June")
  is no longer held back until that month.

- **No war score.** A war now reads as the land each side holds and the great battles won, not a number. The army
  templates are gone from the War Room.
- **War at your rank.** A soldier sees his own corner of the war, a captain his front, a general the whole theatre. Only
  the crown, the Marshal, a commander or a captain in the field opens the War Room. Enemy numbers are guesses below a
  commander. A child has no Arms page, and you hear only of the elite orders news would bring you.
- **No more "initiative".** The pool of free days went with the new Next month and Next year buttons, but its words stayed:
  "60.0 initiative" on the Activities line, "Spend your initiative", "needs 2 initiative" on a market trip, a ⧗ on every
  tile. They are gone. An activity takes days; the Activities line says how tired you are (a little tired, weary, driven
  too hard), and a market trip that is too long says so in days.
- **A feast comes on its day.** The Spring Equinox no longer falls in August, and a scene that opens on "1 May" or on a fair
  is dealt only in that month. "Go to a feast" is shut in a month with none.
- **A fight says what it can end in.** A choice that opens a duel says "a fight to the death" or "a fight, not to the death",
  and a hunt says the beast may kill you.
- **A sick body does not read "hale".** While a fever is on you the health word is "ill with marsh fever".
- **The ending fits the life.** "Master of the Craft" is for a craft; "Survivor" only for a life that began in childhood.
- **One unit.** The stories speak kilometres, as the map and the roads do. No quiet month says the same thing twice in a
  year. The love philtre you could slip into someone's cup is gone.
- **Bodies that make sense.** Strength, speed, endurance, reflexes, wits and will are now a word for where you stand
  among every grown man and woman of your people, measured across the world: most people are "average", a labourer or
  soldier is above it, one in fifty is at either extreme and one in a thousand is off the chart. A child reads as a child
  ("strong for a child of nine"). Another people is also measured against men: a giant's strength is off the chart, and so is
  a giant's slowness. Click a row to see what it is measured against.
- **No more kilograms and milliseconds.** The body is described in words: no "lifts 120 kg", "7.2 m/s", "28 km a day"
  or "reactions 170 ms", and your height is "tall" or "a little short", not centimetres.
- **A skill needs a body (or a mind) to carry it.** Each step of a skill from Journeyman asks a modest baseline of its two
  stats (a Master about an average body or mind for that skill, never a great one). Below it the skill stalls and works
  as the lower step: a sword master with feeble reflexes fights as an Expert. Past it, build your character as you like.
  The world's masters all clear their baselines.
- **The odds you see are the odds that happen.** A check's odds now count the days your will carries a lost cause and the
  days a lapse throws a sure thing, so a long shot shown as a long shot comes up as often as it says.
- "Blades" is called **Swordsmanship** again (it still covers knives and a blade in each hand).
- **Longer lives.** A healthy grown person at peace now dies of nothing much far more rarely, gets over a fever or the
  flux more often, and mostly lives into the sixties or seventies; serfs and slaves a little less, the well fed a little
  more. Plague, wounds, beasts and war still kill.
- **One holder per post.** Every council seat, shire office and army or division command has exactly one holder. A
  promotion hands over a real command: a new General takes an army (its old general moves to the division you left),
  and nobody keeps two seats. Death, prison, deserting or resigning leave every post at once; walking away from a
  command in wartime costs you at court.
- **The Marshal.** In peace the Marshal's seat stands empty and a General leads. When a war needs one (two armies in
  the field, or two enemies at once) the crown names its best General, who takes the main host; at peace he stands
  down. You can be named Marshal only as a General, and then you command every army.
- No more made-up Marshals: above a General stands the real Marshal or the crown. A councillor who loses his seat is
  "once Chancellor", not a second Chancellor; dead lords no longer hold offices for months; new realms get a council.
- Older saves are set straight when they load, with a line for each thing fixed.
- **A Profile page for your character.** The first page of You: your whole standing figure, a card with your name, estate
  and rank, a line about you, and what you are worth in words (body, purse, name, honour); your strength, speed and mind on

- **Arms that look made.** Every weapon and shield a person carries is drawn as the thing it is: an arming sword, a

  own smiths' work.
- **Bows and crossbows are on the figure.** An archer's bow is slung on the back with its quiver, a crossbow with its case
  of bolts; an axe, mace or hammer hangs from the belt; a pole arm stands in the hand.
