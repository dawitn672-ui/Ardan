# Martial concepts by skill (from the old Codex's 303 styles)

*Stamped 2026-11-16 · research lift, design text, no code · feeds `docs/design/growth-standard.md` §3.16 (concepts, grasp,
blind spots; order 59, D-070) and D11+ (`war.md` §14). Source: the Codex's war-and-legends book, 303 styles (87 national,
188 chain, 28 household), 1,250 techniques in five bases (guard 535, strike 478, press 107, feint 77, grapple 53), each
style with a philosophy, strength, **weakness**, school and five school ranks. Style ids (ST..) are the Codex's.*

How these were made: each style's philosophy line and its first technique are a *principle*, not a move. They read as
concepts a person either grasps or does not. Each concept below names the blind spot (what an ungrasped man looks like,
taken from the style's own "weakness"), so the record can say "you have never grasped the measure". Names are mine
where marked (m); the source style shows where the idea came from. Re-skin to the Known World; the Far Lands are out.
Per §3.16 the concepts start at Intermediate; three to six per skill is enough. ST01-01 and ST02-01 are the Known World's own styles (`04-styles.json`), used where they say it best.

## What the Codex also gives that concepts need

- **A teacher who holds the concept** (§3.5, §3.16). Every style names a school, a fee, an entry rule (soldier, gentry,
  any, a race) and a master with a deed, a rival and a weakness: 160 masters. A master teaches the concepts of *his*
  style, and the blind spot in his own weakness is the one he cannot teach (a man with gout cannot show the full drill).
- **Counters as reasons.** All 303 styles state a weakness that is another style's strength: stakes beat the wedge, a
  patient man beats the single cut, a knife beats the wrestler, rain beats the crossbow. That is the "no obviously best
  art" the owner and `lint-choices` want, and it is already written.
- **Five ranks of school** (e.g. Spanner, Bolt of the Rank, File-Leader of the Nine, Captain of Volleys, Master of the
  Ninefold) are titles inside a school, not army ranks (the war book says so). Check them against order 54: several
  use "Marshal" and "General" as *school* titles (ST36-03 "Marshal of the Stair", ST01-01 "General of the Charge"). The
  Known World's own `04-styles.json` has the same, so decide once whether school titles may say "Marshal" (the owner
  removed the army rank, not the word as a school title; unconfirmed).
- **Drills for units** (108 "in the line" entries, such as the Unbroken Volley, the Thousand-Man Square, the Feigned
  Flight, the Closing Ring, the Dyke Hold, the Gate Sally, the Fort Chain, the Caravan Ring): named unit drills with a
  troop code and one sentence. They fit `war.md` §14.2's "about forty drills in eight families" as a source of names and
  one-line rules; group them into the eight families there (standing, shock, loose order, shot, fieldcraft, works,
  the march, the sea).
