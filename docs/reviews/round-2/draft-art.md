# Ardan v2.0.0-beta.1: art direction, character design, VFX and cartography critique

**Lens:** art director, character designer, VFX artist, cartographer. **Build:** `/tmp/ardan/snap/index.html`, driven with Playwright at 420 and 1366 px (and 1920 px), `deviceScaleFactor: 1`.
**Evidence:** about 90 screenshots and contact sheets in `/tmp/ardan/critique/art/shots/`, scripts in `art/scripts/`, running notes in `art/notes/observations.md`. Portraits were drawn straight through `PORTRAIT.svg` (that is, 93-anime-portraits). I rendered **24 random people from a live world, 50 people across every start class with their parents, soldiers made with `A.enlist`, a priest, a priestess and a knight, 96 age steps across 6 peoples, 40 people across the 20 cultures as nobles and as peasants, every wound, quirk, paint, jewel, build, height and face parameter, and a gender quiz.** For the map: the story column, the World tab, and the full map at zooms 1, 2, 3, 6 and 12 in every mode, plus the War Room at three zooms. For battles: the soldier's card, the commander's field (all three views, frame by frame) and the duel. I also captured 16 event cards, 5 cut-ins, the 25 woodblock compositions on every terrain, the icon set, the profile, the family tree, the Chronicle, the death screen and the obituary.

## The verdict in one paragraph

Ardan has one competent, consistent house style: night-blue chrome, Cinzel, flat woodblock skies and cel-shaded busts. In a single screenshot it looks finished. It fails the test the owner set: **it cannot make 20 random people look like 20 different people, and its map does not look like a medieval continent.**

- **The portraits.** Every face comes from one template: the same chibi-anime eyes, nose tick and mouth stroke from age 12 to 80. Identity is carried by hair colour, a hood and a frame colour. Class below the nobility is invisible. Scars almost never happen, and war never leaves a mark.
- **The map.** It is a soft satellite photo with dashed borders. It has no roads, no fords, no bridges and no passes, and its rivers stop in the middle of fields. It carries none of the owner's complaint 8.
- **The battles.** The soldier's battle has no image at all. The commander's battle is boxes on a grey plane, lit by cartoon gunpowder bursts.
- **The anime direction.** It was chosen for "players 13–23" (`docs/design/brief.md`), and it fights the game's own subject: a grim chronicle of serfs, levies and succession. CK3, Battle Brothers, Pentiment and Darkest Dungeon all show that grit and individuality are what make players care about a medieval life.

## How it compares

- **Crusader Kings 3:** per-character DNA (about 70 morph genes) with inheritance, real ageing curves, clothing by culture × rank × era, visible scars, disfigurement and weight. Ardan is about 10% of that range. The data hooks (genes, wounds, culture) exist, but the drawing ignores them.
- **Battle Brothers:** a tiny layered sprite set (head, beard, hair, body, helmet, armour), yet every brother is distinct because the parts are strongly different, with injuries and armour damage shown. The lesson is fewer parts with much bigger variance.
- **Darkest Dungeon:** heavy ink, a limited palette, and silhouette first. Ardan's 32-px silhouettes (`pt-random24-sil.png`) are near-identical head-and-shoulders blobs, with only hair masses differing.
- **Pentiment:** class and region shown entirely through dress and drawing style. This is the closest fit for a chronicle game, and the old style guide pointed there before the anime turn.
- **Inkle (80 Days, Heaven's Vault, Overboard):** one confident graphic hand from map to portrait. Their maps are designed illustrations with routes, not photos.
- **A real medieval continent map** shows roads and itineraries, rivers as the skeleton (with the towns on them), bridges and fords, passes, named forests, marsh, monasteries, ports and sea names, and borders that are visibly different from roads. Ardan shows towns, capitals and dashed lines.

## Keep (good work, do not tear down)

- The rendering discipline is excellent: pure, cached, about 6 KB SVG busts in 0.2–0.4 ms, escaped, with axe passing.
- The woodblock sky system is a lovely base: the hour table, bokashi, interior light and the place cartouche.
- The obituary's dusk print with candle and crow is the best image in the game.
- The duel's face-off layout (two full figures and the "measure" line) is right.
- The captain view's soldier sprites per company is the right direction for battle readability.
- The War Room's tooltip ("Hills · width 55 · attacker ×0.78") makes terrain legible.
- The Chronicle's vellum page with red initials is good.
- The icon set is consistent in weight and grid.

## Top 5 to do first

1. **A face-gene system with real variance and inheritance** (#1, #2, #5, #6). Extend `p.app` to 15+ continuous genes, blend them from the parents, widen every range 4–6×, add sex dimorphism and continuous ageing, and accept it only when a blind 20-person sheet with the hair covered is told apart.
2. **A dress table by class × culture × wealth** (#3, #25, #26, #19, #27). Undyed rags to fur and scarlet, the soldier's layer visible in the bust, livery only for retainers, hoods capped by context. Test with one sheet of all 8 statuses × 4 cultures, labels hidden: a viewer must name each class.
3. **Make the body record its life** (#4, #23, #34). Battle and duel wounds write `p.wounds` with a location. NPC veterans are seeded with scars. Build and height drive the silhouette.
4. **A cartographer's pass on the map** (#8, #9, #14, #15, #17). A road graph with fords, bridges and passes; rivers from source to sea with towns on them; vector woods and marsh; a distinct border style; a 12-hue realm palette; names for seas, ranges and rivers. It is the visible half of complaint 8.
5. **One art bible and a battle that looks like a battle** (#7, #10, #11, #12, #13, #31). Pick the woodcut-colour-print hand (Pentiment × Hiroshige), re-skin the cut-ins and portraits toward it, give the soldier's battle phase vignettes, and draw terrain, troop glyphs and period FX on the commander's field.
