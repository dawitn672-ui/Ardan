## Each, in words

Dates are the lore's (the year is 1105). Where a line is new, it is built on what the lore already holds, and the source is named.

**I · Winter Wheat** · longsword, 1,155 mm · Empire of Aldmark.
*Made:* at Irondeep by Clan Brightanvil in 951, the dwarves' seal on the Iron Road Contract (the lore: King Arnulf sealed it with Harald Ironside by torchlight). 154 years old. *History:* iron for wheat: the black heart is hold-iron, the gold roots are Aldmark's wheat. Carried in both Horde Wars. *Owner:* the imperial crown, as a treasure of the realm. *Bearer:* the Marshal of Aldmark while the seat is filled (in 1105 Leofric Stane); otherwise it hangs over the iron ring in the Oath Hall. This fits D-017 (the Marshal's seat stays empty until needed) and the lore's Marshal alike. *Why:* the Oath Moot takes it down and hangs it up, so an emperor who wants a great war must ask for the sword. *In the hand:* broad and point-heavy; learned, it shears a common blade at the bind and goes through mail; a quick man who is gone when it falls has nothing to fear. *Art:* Under the Snow. *In the portrait:* planted before the Marshal, both hands on the pommel.

**II · The Last Page** · greatsword, 1,670 mm · Holy See of Solenne.
*Made:* at Solenne in 760 from the swords the last imperial guards laid down when they chose vows over the axe (the founding of the Knights of the Sun); gilded by the women of House Corvane, who judge the Temple's gold leaf. 345 years old. *History:* the knights' names are in the Book of Noon; the Grand Masters' are on the blade. Twenty-three are written and there is room for eleven more. *Owner:* the Temple. *Bearer:* the Grand Master (Bruno Castell), on Return Day and in the field. *Why:* a Knight of the Sun gives up house and arms, so the order has no heirlooms; and every Valdane king is crowned kneeling under it, which is the Temple's hold on Varenna. *In the hand:* a bearing sword as long as a man; few can fight with it; one who can clears a ring two paces wide; get inside the ring and it cannot reach you. *Art:* The Sun Does Not Turn Back. *In the portrait:* carried upright at the shoulder in both fists.

**III · Last Well** · sabre, 950 mm · Confederacy of the Sand Clans.
*Made:* the blade at Chanari about 960 by one of the first twelve smiths of the Compact; hilted at Mirah. About 145 years old. *History:* 23 rungs in the steel for the 23 clans, the last filled with gold for Mirah, the last well on the Salt Road; Muhsin Sayf carried it through the siege of 1060. *Owner:* Clan Sayf (it is the curved sword on the clan's arms). *Bearer:* Zayd Blackveil, outlaw, with no right to it. *Why:* it went into the desert with him in 1098; Harith Sayf calls it stolen and sends no one after it, because a chief who can call the hand a thief's can call the war on Zanara's road camps not his. *In the hand:* light, cuts where a straight sword must be drawn back; little point and a small guard. *Art:* False Water. *In the portrait:* drawn, laid across the body to the far shoulder.

**IV · Held Breath** · dagger, 390 mm · Kingdom of Greyhaven.
*Made:* cast in fleet bronze before 719, a chain-diver's work knife; hilted in silver and pearl for King Cedric in 761. Close on 400 years old. *History:* Wenna Tidewell freed the harbor chain in 719 with it in her teeth, and her teeth are in the grip; Osric the Diver had it at Yarbek in 882. *Owner:* House Tidewell, as an heirloom of the blood, never a crown jewel. *Bearer:* the heir (Prince Tristan). *Why:* by the Ring Law of 916 the heir who dives for the ring comes up owning the knife. Tristan's dive was helped (the lore's secret), so the knife is the proof of a right he may not have. *In the hand:* short, broad, will not rust, bend or snap; in a clinch or under water nothing is better; against a sword in the open its legend does not lengthen it. *Art:* The Long Breath. *In the portrait:* in his right hand; his left has gone to the silver ring at his throat, as the lore says it does.

**V · Thaw** · war axe, 1,460 mm · Kingdom of Northreach.
*Made:* at Kingsmere about 810 for the exile Eirik Winterborn, by a smith of the Fairrush court. The haft was its fourth. 295 years old. *History:* Eirik the Ice-Walker broke the sea gate of Wolfhaven with it in January 812, after crossing the frozen bay. Nine kings were raised on the shields with it; the tenth was raised without it. *Owner:* Northreach's crown claims it. *Bearer:* no one: it is **lost**. King Bjorn III had it beside his chair at Kingsmere the night he was poisoned in 1090. *Why:* Aldmark says it was never in the hall. King Eirik III's ten years of silence over his father's murder (the lore's secret letter of 1093) have run out, and whoever shows the axe names the murderer's house. *In the hand:* long, thin-bladed, quick; splits a shield, hooks an ankle; inside its swing it is a staff. *Art:* Ice Underfoot. *In the portrait:* drawn as last carried, by Bjorn III.

**VI · The Latch** · war hammer, 1,000 mm · Kingdom of Irondeep.
*Made:* the head by Sigmund Brightanvil in 612, the year the first forge was lit; the haft remade six times. 493 years old. *History:* three doors: iron, stone and steel, and dwarves; this is the last door's latch. A ring on the haft for every Keeper, filled with red on the day they die (as the names on the wall of the Last Hall are painted red): eighteen red, one bare. *Owner:* the Last Door, not the queen and no clan. *Bearer:* the Keeper, for life; the head passes from the dead to the newly sworn. *Why:* the oath of 612, "Not past me"; and whichever of Queen Brynja and Prince Korrin the Keeper stands behind has the hold. *In the hand:* all weight in the head; folds plate; slow to lift; a long spear keeps it out all day. *Art:* Not Past Me. *In the portrait:* at the port across the Keeper's body.

**VII · Grandfather** · spear, 2,076 mm · Empire of Zanara.
*Made:* at Zanara in 930 for Mosi the Runner's three hundred; blade by the smiths of Kabale, gold from the Ancestor Hill. 175 years old. *History:* the face on the socket is the first ancestor of the Kanemba line; the Gold Masks say Grandfather goes first. It is the only face among them that is still solid gold (the lore: the masks were melted in 1062 and are bronze under gold leaf). *Owner:* the throne. *Bearer:* nobody: it is **in pawn** to House Sorondo, in their strongroom; the First Face parades a copy. *Why:* the Salt War was paid for in secret with the Masks' gold; if the bankers show the spear the empire learns it, so the crown keeps paying. *In the hand:* a stabbing spear with a forearm of blade and a heavy foot; beat the point aside and it is a stick. *Art:* Before the Point Arrives. *In the portrait:* in two iron brackets on the strongroom wall, Kwame Sorondo beside it.

**VIII · Midsummer** · glaive, 2,080 mm · League of the Tusk Clans.
*Made:* at Grakmar in 1062 from the spearheads of thirty-one clans; the horn is that year's rhino's. 43 years old, the youngest of the ten. *History:* Grakka Ironhide killed the year's rhino alone on midsummer's day 1062 and thirty-one clans swore to her; she died of the sun at the Great Ride of 1080 with it in her hand. *Owner:* the War-Mother's hearth, which by eastern custom goes to the youngest daughter. *Bearer:* Urza Ironhide. *Why:* the clans swear over the rhino a War-Mother has killed. Urza fainted in 1104 and her heir froze at her trial (the lore's secrets); if that is learned, the glaive goes to whoever takes the next rhino. *In the hand:* made to stop a ton of rhino; what it hits is opened or broken; slow coming back. *Art:* The Year's Rhino. *In the portrait:* held upright at her side.

**IX · The Unbending** · longbow, 1,990 mm · Kingdom of Sylvaran.
*Made:* cut on the Yew Ridges and worked at Evenglade about 250 BR for Maerith, then fourteen. Some 1,350 years old. *History:* in 291 Queen Maerith the Unbending loosed the first arrow of the War of the Forest Edge with it ("let them bring their sun; we will be its shadow"). The bow has her name; three archers have strung it since she died, and none was let keep it. *Owner:* the Oakborn line. *Bearer:* no one: it hangs **unused**. Queen Ithrien's left hand is still and cannot hold a bow. *Why:* it goes to the Oakborn heir, and the only other of the blood is Thalion Halfleaf, an archer the law bars from the crown; to hand him the bow is to name him. *In the hand:* draws like a ship's cable; drawn true it goes through a shield at two hundred paces; the arrow must still find its man. *Art:* The Sun's Shadow. *In the portrait:* unstrung on two pegs, Thalion under it with his hands behind him.

**X · Paymaster** · crossbow, 815 mm · Highland League.
*Made:* at Roseham in 952 for the first Captain-General; its steel bow is its second, of Irondeep steel, fitted in 1052 after the horn one split at Hartgate. 153 years old. *History:* held the stakes at Hartgate in 1051 with its last bolt. The horn plates on the tiller carry a tally, one cut for each contract paid in full: 206 cuts, three plates blank. *Owner:* the Silver Chest, the valleys in common. *Bearer:* the Captain-General (Ulric Greycloak), by the Council's gift. *Why:* since 952 a hire is sealed when its bolt is shot into the hirer's gatepost and ended when the pay is counted and the bolt drawn. Ulric took Aldmark's silver before the Council voted (the lore's secret), and the valleys are asking where the bolt is. *In the hand:* three shots to a bowman's twelve, each through plate at a hundred paces; between shots, eight pounds of wood. *Art:* The Gorge Shot. *In the portrait:* slung on his back, the steel bow behind his shoulders.

## How a piece is built

A legendary is a **record of parts**, in millimetres, along its own axis (`u` from hilt or butt toward the point, `v` across it). The record names its parts in the order a maker would fit them, and each part is drawn by a shared drawer:

| Layer of the record | What it is | Drawer (`src/kit.mjs`, `src/fine.mjs`) |
|---|---|---|
| Outline | The blade, head, stave or stock: stations along the axis, its own shoulders, barbs, horns | `blade`, `backblade` (one edge, through a bent frame), `piece`, `tube`, `haft` |
| Metal | One of the finishes: bright, white, watered, black, dwarf-steel, bronze, gold, red gold, silver | the ramps in `MET`; `TURN` (how a form turns from its lit edge to its far one) |
| Heart | A core of another steel down the blade, with its watering | `blade({ core })`, `watering` |
| Inlay and writing | Veins like roots, vines, plaits, key patterns, lettering in a wire hand, ornament like script | `roots`, `vineOn`, `plait`, `fret`, `lettering`, `inlay`, `engrave` |
| Fittings | Guard, grip and its binding, collars, pommel, socket, langets, bands | `piece`, `winding`, `xwrap`, `ridge`, `beads`, `trim` (one metal edging another) |
| Stones | Cut stones in a collet, cabochons, a pearl, enamel | `gem`, `cabochon`, `pearl`, `enamel` |
| Soft things | Pelt, hair, tassel, cord | `pelt`, `tufts`, `tassel` |
| Its years | A nick with a date, scratches, clouds in the polish, salt, a bare ring | options on `blade`; drawn by the record itself |
| Light | One glint where the polish is highest | `glint` |

- **One record, every size.** The same record draws the plate, the closer looks, the flat strip and the piece on the figure. A **frame** puts its axis anywhere on a page at any angle (`frame(G, deg)`), exactly as the figure library's own `swordArt(S, G, a, L, …)` takes a point and a direction; `arcFrame` bends it for a sabre or a bow. Paint that depends on the light (which face of a blade is the lit one) is worked out from the frame, so a piece can be turned freely.
- **Level of detail.** `D.lod` is 2 on a plate and 1 on a figure. At 1 a record leaves out its engraving, writing, watering and wear and keeps outline, metals, accent and glint (growth.md §4: "at 46 px its outline, accent and one glint"). Shapes smaller than half a pixel are dropped by the scene itself.
- **Never changes.** Every irregularity (the wander of a vein, the watering, a scratch) comes from a stream seeded by the piece's id (`stream(id, channel)`, the figure library's own). Nothing calls `Math.random` or the clock. Rendering reads nothing and writes nothing.
- **The scene is the game's.** Drawing goes through `src/ui/engine/figure/scene.js` and `g.js`, imported and not changed, so a piece is the same kind of SVG, with the same line and the same light, as a person.

## On the figure: how each registers as gear

Today `wardrobe.js` holds one row per item id (`GEAR_ART`), and `registerGear(id, row)` adds one; a row names the drawn piece (`art: 'sword'`) and its make and rarity, and `gear.js` holds the item functions that draw each piece on the rig (`ITEMS`). A legendary needs one more kind of row and one more item, and nothing else:

```js
// E2, when a unique is created or loaded (data/items/uniques.json):
registerGear('winter_wheat', { art: 'legend', u: 'winter-wheat', carry: 'plant' });

// gear.js, one item for every legendary: it stands the piece's record on the rig at its carry point
ITEMS.legend = (C, o) => { /* frame(G, deg) from the rig and o.carry; record.draw({ S, F, lod: 1, … }) into the layer */ };
```

The page does exactly this, at run time and without touching `src/`: `src/figure.mjs` adds `ITEMS.legend` and six stances to the library's own tables, and each portrait is `compose(look, { crop })` with `{ id: 'legend', u, mode, … }` as the last item on the person's list of gear. The piece is drawn into its own small scene and laid into one of the figure's layers, so it gets the figure's depth shadow and light pass with everything else.

| Piece | `carry` | Where the frame sits on the rig | Figure layer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winter Wheat | `plant` | point on the ground before the feet, blade upright; both wrists sent to the pommel | `propFront` |
| The Last Page | `bear` | upright at the right shoulder, the cross a hand above the upper fist | `propFront` |
| Last Well | `across` | from the right fist at the hip, across the body to the far shoulder | `propFront` |
| Held Breath | `raise` | in the right fist at the chest, point up | `propFront` |
| Thaw, Midsummer | `pole` | butt on the ground beside the right foot, the haft through the fist | `propFront` |
| The Latch | `port` | across the body in both fists, head by the right shoulder | `propFront` |
| Grandfather, The Unbending | `stand` | not held: on brackets or pegs beside or above the person | `back` |
| Paymaster | `back` | slung: behind the shoulders, its strap the figure's own baldric | `propBack` |

What E2 would still have to build: the carry point for a piece that is **sheathed** (each sword and the dagger want their own scabbards drawn; only the drawn carries are shown here), the choice of carry from the person's stance, and the cache key (`u:<id>` joins the portrait's key, as economy-and-items.md §2.4 already says).

Against growth.md §4's budgets (12 shapes at 46 px, 60 on a card, 220 at full size; 8 ms for a plate):

- **Compose is cheap**: 3 to 9 ms a plate once the code is warm (the first two rows carry the page's warm-up), 1 to 8 ms at figure scale.
- **Shape counts at full size are near the budget** (86 to 297 against 220; a shape here is one path, and a path may hold a whole net of inlay).
- **At figure scale they are over it** (66 to 244 against 60 on a card). The records thin themselves at `lod 1`, but not yet by enough: a third level for list sizes (silhouette, two metals, the accent) is the next cut and is easy, because the parts are already ranked.
- **Paint is over the 8 ms budget for a plate** (25 to 100 ms at twice the size). A plate is painted once and kept, as portraits are (D-036 item 4), so this is a one-time cost a piece, but it is real.
- The card's paint (90 to 155 ms) is the figure library's own light and depth passes, as gate 2 measured; the piece adds 5 to 35 ms of it.

## An honest reading

**What is good.** The ten are ten different things: outline, metal and ornament tell the realm before the plate is read, and each beats its ordinary cousin at a glance in the strip under it. Winter Wheat carries the owner's example in its bones (dark heart, bright bevels, gold roots, clawed guard, cut stone and pommel). The histories hang on what the lore already says, and the ownership of each is a live question in 1105, which is what makes a treasure worth playing for. The pieces sit on the figure library's people without a seam: same scene, same light, same line, drawn from one record.

**Where it falls short.**

1. **It is still clean vector work, not paint.** The example has depth, grain and a hand in it. These have gradients, crisp bands of light and fine line; metal is convincing at arm's length and plain close up. The guard of Winter Wheat is flatter and simpler than the example's sculpted one.
2. **Sculpted forms are few.** The ancestor's face on Grandfather and the horn on Midsummer are the only modelled things. Animal heads, figures and pierced work (which legendary pieces of other kinds will want) need hand-authored outlines; one earlier study (a wolf's head pommel, in `sketches/`) shows how far a first try gets, which is not far enough.
3. **Long thin pieces are thin.** The bow, the spear and the glaive are true to scale, so the eye goes to the closer looks. The plates lean on them.
4. **On the figure the pieces are small and not yet thinned enough** (see the costs). Only drawn carries are shown: no scabbards. The hands are the library's two shapes (a relaxed hand and a fist), so a grip on a haft is a fist laid over it. The stances are the library's front-facing ones with new wrist targets, so nothing is swung, shouldered or drawn back.
5. **Three of the portraits are not "in the bearer's hands"** because the story says so: one lost (drawn with its last bearer), one in pawn (on a strongroom wall), one unused (on its pegs).
6. **The writing on two pieces is in a plain wire hand** (straight strokes and arcs), right for an inlaid blade but not a scribe's hand; The Last Page's names want a proper book hand at full size.
7. **Not done:** each piece's scabbard, quiver or case; the back of any piece; a night version of the page; a playtester agent's pass (a subagent cannot start one).
