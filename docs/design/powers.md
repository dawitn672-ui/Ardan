# Powers: one scale for every being (wave 1)

**The owner, 2026-11-25:** "increase the difficulty of the world by adding countless beast and mythical beings ... the
strongest warrior in the ardan world before this update should be weak when masters of other races and actual monsters
appear ... make the powerscaling of this world much larger." Later the same day: levels, not tier names; a profile of
capabilities, so beings of one level fight differently; intelligence classes; full characters for the great beings;
titles as data with buffs (their requirements never shown); ranks up to SSS; some kinds bloodthirsty by nature, with no
calming choice; legends centuries to ages old; "unique" only for what is one of a kind; titles a being is born with; no
gods as a class: the summit is the King of Dragons, the Primordial Archdemons and the Sword Saint, a man. The genre's
ideas are used; every name here is original.

Code: `src/world/9999970-powers/` (README there). Data: `data/power.json`, `data/bestiary.json` (generated by
`tools/powers/gen-bestiary.mjs`, never edited by hand), `data/legends.json`.

## 1. The level

One number, **might**, and the **level** read from it: `level = 10 x log10(might / 0.01)`, 1 to 130. Every ten levels
is ten times the might, so the ladder is huge (a rat at 2, the King of Dragons at 107: 10^10.5 times the might).

- **A person's might** is his fighting worth in conscripts (`A.V2S.shownConscripts`, the number his sheet already shows),
  squared. Squared because Lanchester's square law makes the fighting strength of a band of N equal men grow as N^2:
  a man worth twenty conscripts has the strength of that band. Nothing about people changed; only the scale above them grew.
- **A beast of the release** (the 25 rows of `A.BEASTS`) is put on the same scale by its own duel row: the human fit
  `ln(conscripts) = -2.244 + 0.0519 x worth` (656 humans, seed 7) on its duel worth, times the square root of its blood
  over a man's, squared. The test checks the data against the live rows (none off by more than a level).
- **Every other being** has its level written in data, its might read from it.

Measured on worlds 7, 11, 23, 41 (test `pwLadder`):

**Ranks** are words over the levels (`data/power.json` bands): G 1, F 10, E 18, D 26, C 34, B 42, A 52, S 60, SS 70,
SSS 80, and "beyond every rank" from 100, held only by those four. The faiths' gods are not on the scale (`myth`).

**Odds.** `p(A beats B) = 1 / (1 + (mightB / mightA)^0.55)`, then the profile's matchup. k = 0.55 because one of the
release's fighter tiers (about 14 worth, one e-fold of conscripts) wins three fights in four. The player sees only words
(`A.RN.oddsWord`): the best man of a world against a giant king reads "very long odds", against a legend "odds no one would take".

## 2. The profile: the level is not the whole

Ten capabilities, 0 to 10 (12 for the summit): strength, speed, hide and armour, venom, flight, regeneration, resistance
to magic, numbers, cunning, magic. `A.POWER.compare` adds log-odds for each matchup and names it in words: a flyer
against a man with no bow (-0.9, "it flies, and nothing you carry reaches the sky"), hide against strength, venom against
a hardy body, regeneration against no magic, numbers, magic against a ward, cunning. A person's profile comes from his
stats and armour. Test `pwProfiles`: at level 3 a bat (flight, numbers) gives a peasant 0.34, a slime 0.72.

**Titles** are data with `buffs` (capabilities and level) and honour; `A.POWER.being` reads them, so a title visibly
makes a being stronger. Some are born (`born: true`: "Primordial Archdemon", "Old Blood" of the ancient dragons,
"Gate-Born"); the rest carry `req: "hidden"` and the player never sees what earned them. The player's own titles are a
separate design (with skills).

## 3. The bestiary: 270 kinds

19 families (vermin, wolves and hounds, great cats, bears and apes, boars and great hooved beasts, serpents, wyrms and
drakes, dragons, wild giants and trolls, ogres, harpies and griffons and great birds, spiders and insects, the restless
dead, spirits and elementals, demons, monsters of the deep, the fey, slimes and oozes, constructs), 90 lines, 88 with
evolution, levels 2 to 76. Each kind: level, profile, intelligence, habitats (the map's seven terrains and the coast),
diet, active seasons, what it leaves (with a worth in suns, for the economy to come), the step to its next form, and a
**behaviour** profile: drives, social (solitary to host), how it hunts, its time of day, its mating season, what it
guards, what provokes it and what turns it, how it treats people, livestock and other kinds, its daily and yearly habit,
and how it differs by ground. The 46 LORE creatures that are real beasts keep their ids (`cre`), and `A.BEASTS` is untouched.

**Intelligence:** beast (instinct only), cunning (ambush, memory; no bargaining), speaking (bargains, lies, grudges;
decides through A.WHY), wise (plans for years). 117 beast, 34 cunning, 92 speaking, 27 wise (apex forms take a mind).

**Natures.** 21 kinds act from a nature, not a reason of the moment: bloodthirsty (the pyre hounds: "kills past hunger
and leaves the meat; cannot be bargained with"), relentless (the restless dead), hungry beyond reason (ghouls: "flees
only fire"), mindless (swarms, slimes), cruel (gate horrors). Their nature is their reason (rule 18), and the card offers
no calming choice. Individuals vary by a seeded temper (bold, wary, sly, old, young, scarred, starving, unhurried).

**Evolution.** Up to 24 notable monsters live in the wild provinces. Each spring they feed (four tries a year, the herds
of a settled province for a stock-raider), fight (rivals and hunters; a loss kills a third of the time), and rise to the
next form when they have fed, won and lived long enough at this one (`evolve: { feeds, wins, years }`, higher forms ask
more). The record names why: "rat-matron -> warren-king: fed 22 times on the herds of Brookcliff, won 1 fight, 7 years".
At the top a monster takes a name and a mind ("Ironclaw the Warren King") and, if it speaks, chooses through A.WHY
whether to raid. About thirty rises in forty years (`pwEvolution`).

## 4. The great beings

15, each one holder, each a character sheet: age (centuries to ages), history, traits, values, fears, desires, loves
and grudges toward named realms, peoples and other beings (with the reason), speech, habits by season, what it wants
this century, and how it answers being sought, flattered, attacked or ignored. Homes vary: lair, roam, a city (Morvane,
unknown), a realm's service (the Iron Warden), the sea (Tessaly), sealed (Ghazrul).

Each season a legend decides through `A.WHY.decide` (kind `legend`): sleep, hunt, roam, stay, march (an oath to a crown
at war), or raid a province. The reasons come from the sheet and the world: its habit for the season, its hunger, its
desires (the wealth of a town, books, horses, souls), its grudges and loves toward the province's realm and people, its
fears (a realm's host), and the cost of waking the realms. A raid costs the province people and the realm stability, is
news near the player (a rumour far away, three times in ten), and the crown answers through A.WHY: send a host (its
reckoning is `A.POWER.compare` of a host, N^1.35 x 2, against the being) or endure. A host can kill a legend; a man cannot.

**The summit sleeps.** The King of Dragons (wounded under the Crown Range), Ghazrul (bound under the Burning Waste) and
the Sword Saint (stone on his ridge) decide once a year whether what would wake them has come (the King's daughter slain,
the Saint stirring, the Archdemons loose, the wars' blood on the seal); sleep weighs 3.5, and one that woke within thirty
years sleeps on. A waking lasts three years; if the King and the Saint are both awake they meet on the ridge.

**The Sword Saint**, Cael Vardan: a shepherd's son who learned the sword for a sack of oats, bound Ghazrul, cut Morvane's
first face from him and brought down the King of Dragons on the Saint's Stair nine hundred years ago, then sat down on the
ridge and turned to stone. Every child hears his story at about four, told as his people tell it (human, elf, dwarf, orc,
giant tellings differ), with one of his sayings; his sword Morrowglass, his rivals and the places named for him are on his
sheet and in the codex. Whether he died or sleeps: he sleeps (the priests' telling is true), so the King can wake him.

## 5. The world pushes back, by choice

Each month the ground may bring a card (`pw_encounter`): a chance by terrain (plains 0.25% a month to mountains 1%),
times 0.3 in a city, 0.6 in a town, 1.2 in the wild, 4 when travelling, exploring or on pilgrimage this month, 1.5 for a
soldier at war, 1.3 in winter; at most one in four months. The kind met is drawn by habitat and season around the place's
level (8 in a city, 22 in the wild, more in the mountains and marshes, more on the road). Over forty years a city life
meets about 0.8 cards, a wild one about 3.7, a wild one on the road about 15 (`pwSettledSafe`).

The card says what it is, why it came (its season, hunger, ground, young; or its nature), how it stands against you in
words with both levels, its strongest edge, whether it can think. Choices: fight, flee, try what turns its kind (only if
something does), speak (only if it speaks and has no nature against it). Each choice shows odds in words. A fight within
eight levels goes to the duel engine; beyond that it is settled at once: a kill, or a wound, or death (`A.mortalCheck`
with a penalty that grows with the gap). The hunt (`A.startHunt`) takes 15% of ordinary hunts and 60% of famous ones
from the bestiary, by place and the hunter's level (prey a few levels below him; famous quarry above, a named monster nearby
first). A great being on the player's ground can be met (`pw_great`) and answers as its sheet says, through A.WHY.

The world's own draws (whether a card comes, the monsters' and legends' lives) use a seeded stream of their own, so the
game's main stream moves only when the player meets something.
