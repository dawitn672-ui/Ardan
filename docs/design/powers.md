**The owner, 2026-11-25:** "increase the difficulty of the world by adding countless beast and mythical beings ... the
strongest warrior in the ardan world before this update should be weak when masters of other races and actual monsters
appear ... make the powerscaling of this world much larger." Later the same day, in order (the newest wins): levels, not
tier names; a profile of capabilities, so beings of one level fight differently; intelligence classes; full characters for
the great beings; titles as data with buffs, requirements never shown, some had from birth; ranks up to SSS; some kinds
bloodthirsty by nature, with no calming choice; legends centuries to ages old; "unique" only for what is one of a kind; no
gods; exact level anchors (below); each level worth more than the one before; fighting power is the level's might times
titles and mastery; the dragons live in the Dragon Mountains; then (2026-11-26) Sarnavoth at a slight disadvantage against
the three Primordial Archdemons together, the Saint (700) even with one of them (770-780) through his class Sword Saint,
skills at most level 10 (past it they evolve or merge), and full sheets with skills, items and titles for every legend and master;
no demon kingdom; places kept as data keyed to terrain and named places, for the map to come. Then order 92 (2026-11-26):

more monsters and creatures to fit in between" (D-185: every legend's level x 1000/800); and order 93 the same day stretched

## 1. Might, level, power

- **Might** is one number. A person's is his fighting worth in conscripts (`A.V2S.shownConscripts`, the number his sheet
  already shows), squared: Lanchester's square law makes a band of N equal men N^2 strong, so a man worth twenty
  conscripts has that band's strength. Nothing about people's might changed; only the levels read from it. A beast of the
  release (the 25 rows of `A.BEASTS`) gets its might from its own duel row through the human fit
  `ln(conscripts) = -2.244 + 0.0519 x worth` (656 humans, seed 7) times the root of its blood over a man's, squared; the
  test checks the data against the live rows. Every other being's level is written in data and its might read from it.
- **A person's level** is his own since D-181 (`src/people/9999975-level`): seeded at the level read from his might, then
  raised by defeats; his might (and so the odds) stays his fighting worth.
- **Level** (1 to 1000) is read from the might: `log10(might) = -0.637 + 0.005858 L + 0.0000015 L^2` above level 20,
  fifteen levels to a tenfold below (order 93's curve; order 84's was `-0.695 + 0.009746 L + 0.000002 L^2`). Because the
  square term is positive, each level multiplies the might by more than the one before (1.0138 a level at 25, 1.0171 at
  500, 1.0206 at 1000), so 650 is 2.4 times 600 and 1000 about 51 times 800. Fitted to the owner's anchors of order 93
  (the median grown human at 35 and a soldier's might 1-2 at 106-154 fix the slope; the square term then puts the
  strongest of a world near 500-550 and the grown dragons at 700 without a second knot):

- **Fighting power** is the level's might times its titles' power (`buffs.power`), its items' power (`gives.power`)
  and its **art**: `2^(art / 5)`, where art is the sum over its fight skills of the rank's weight (D 1, C 2, B 4, A 8,
  S 16: master prompt 9's power budget) times level / 10. Skills stop at level 10; past it they only evolve or merge, so a
  great being's strength is in the rank of its skills, its titles and its arms. A person's skills are already in his
  fighting worth, so they count once. A few great beings fighting together add up nearly in a line (the sum of their powers, times 1.05 for each past the first); a host of many men keeps Lanchester's law (`PW.hostOf`: N^1.35 x a soldier), since every spear of a host reaches the foe at once and a handful of giants mostly get in each other's way.
  Titles split the kinds of strength: the Dragon King's and the Archdemons' titles carry raw **firepower** (area, breath,
  ruin), the Sword Saint's class and title carry **single combat**; the profile carries both, and the matchup reads them
  (firepower breaks numbers; a duellist's art finds the gap one to one). Test `pwPowerNotLevel` holds the three matchups.
  Inside the code `t = 10 x log10(power / 0.01)` is the even scale gaps and draws use.
- **Ranks** are words over the levels (order 93): G 1, F 20 (common folk), E 80 (a soldier), D 230 (a knight), C 300 (an
  elite, the lower demons), B 420 (a kingdom's champion), A 500 (the strongest of the peoples), S 640 (the great demons, a
  grown dragon), SS 740 (the strong dragons and the calamities), SSS 800 (the legends).
  The faiths' gods are not on the scale (`myth`).
- **Odds:** `p(A beats B) = 1 / (1 + (powerB / powerA)^0.55)`, then the profile's matchup. k = 0.55 because one of the
  release's fighter tiers (about 14 worth, one e-fold of conscripts) wins three fights in four. The player sees only words.

## 2. The profile and titles

Twelve capabilities, 0 to 10 (12 for the legends): strength, speed, hide and armour, venom, flight, regeneration,
resistance to magic, numbers, cunning, magic, firepower, single combat. `A.POWER.compare` adds log-odds for each matchup and names it: a flyer against a man
with no bow (-0.9, "it flies, and nothing you carry reaches the sky"), hide against strength, venom against a hardy body,
regeneration against no magic, numbers, magic against a ward, cunning. A person's profile comes from his stats and armour.

**Titles** are data with `buffs` (capabilities, level, power) and honour; `A.POWER.being` reads them, so a title visibly
makes a being stronger. Some are born (`born: true`: "Primordial Archdemon", "Old Blood" of the ancient dragons,
"Gate-Born", "Dragon-Born of the First Clutch"); the rest carry `req: "hidden"`. Since D-183 every title lives in `data/titles/` (the legends' and masters' sheets name theirs by id, read as before: nothing moved) beside the titles people earn (`src/people/9999976-titles`); *the Sword Saint* is both Cael's title and a class a person may earn when he is gone.

## 3. The bestiary: 301 kinds

19 families, 107 lines (98 with evolution), levels 1 to 790 (with the Old Blood). Each kind: level, profile, intelligence, habitats (the map's
seven terrains, the coast, and named places where the canon fixes one: the dragons' "the Dragon Mountains"), diet, active
seasons, what it leaves (worth in suns, for the economy to come), the step to its next form, and a behaviour profile:
drives, social, how it hunts, time of day, mating season, what it guards, what provokes and what turns it, how it treats
people, livestock and other kinds, its daily and yearly habit, how it differs by ground. The 46 LORE creatures that are
real beasts keep their ids (`cre`); `A.BEASTS` is untouched.

**The high band (orders 92 and 93, D-185; set by nature in D-191).** D-185 added 29 kinds in eleven new lines to fill the
levels around the grown dragons and up to the weakest legend (800), and stretched every older kind by about 1.55, which put
apex animals, trolls and ogres among the dragons. Orders 95 and 96 (D-191) set every level by nature instead: each kind has
a `tier` (animal, great beast, monster, drake, giant, undead, spirit, fey, construct, young, grown, elder or ancient dragon,
lower, great or ancient demon, calamity), the lines are written in the owner's levels (`lv`), and from 600 to 800 stand only
the 45 calamities. The dragons of the High Roost are removed (archived in `archive/bestiary-high-roost/`); the three
ancient dragons are Ash, Frost and Storm, each one of a kind on its own line; four ancient demon kinds (33 individuals)
stand with the elder dragons. Counted kinds carry `world` and the dragons' family a `cap` of 100, which the wilds keep
(`A.POWER.room`). The release's 25 beasts take their level by nature too; their duel rows stay as `duelT`. Every kind from
500 to 800 and every kind that moved, with its reason: `docs/design/bestiary-bands.md`.

**Intelligence:** beast (instinct), cunning (ambush, memory, no bargaining), speaking (bargains, grudges; decides through
A.WHY), wise (plans for years). **Natures:** 21 kinds act from their nature (bloodthirsty, relentless, mindless, cruel,
hungry beyond reason, each with a written spec such as "kills past hunger and leaves the meat; cannot be bargained with");
their card has no calming choice, and the nature is their reason (rule 18). Individuals vary by a seeded temper.

**Evolution.** Up to 24 notable monsters live in the wild provinces; each spring they feed, fight and rise to the next form
once fed, victorious and old enough at this one; the record names why. At the top a monster takes a name, a title and a mind
and, if it speaks, chooses through A.WHY whether to raid. About thirty rises in forty years (`pwEvolution`).

## 4. The legends

16 legends, each one of a kind, each holding a title, each with skills (rank and level, what each does, what it evolved
or merged from), items (quality and what they give) and a class where it has one, each the strongest of its kind on the continent and a threat to
nations. Each has a full sheet: age (centuries to ages), history, traits, values, fears, desires, loves and grudges toward
named realms, peoples and other beings (with the reason), speech, habits by season, what it wants this century, how it
answers being sought, flattered, attacked (challenged) or ignored, and its mastery. Homes: lair, roam, a city (Noir's
shadow, unknown), a crown's service (the Iron Warden), the sea (Tessaly), sealed (Red); each with terrains and a named place
so the final map can place it in one pass. Since order 98 every demon lives in Hell: Noir holds the Gatelands and the
Seventh Gate (his shadow keeps a house in a town of men), Red rules the Cinderlands behind the Glass Seal in the Burning
Waste, Blanc rules the Hush and walks the pilgrim roads in spring.

Each season a legend decides through `A.WHY.decide` (kind `legend`): sleep, hunt, roam, stay, march (an oath to a crown at
war), or raid a province, for reasons from its sheet and the world (habit, hunger, desires, grudges and loves toward the
province's realm and people, fears, the cost of waking the realms). A raid costs people and stability, is news near the
player and a rumour far away, and the crown answers through A.WHY: send a host (its reckoning is `compare` of a host,
N^1.35 x 2, against the legend) or endure. Legends can be challenged: by a host (rarely, and it usually breaks), by the
player meeting one (`pw_great`: "Draw steel"), and by each other.

Two sleep: Sarnavoth, King of Dragons, wounded under the highest peak of the Dragon Mountains since the Saint threw him
there sixty years ago, and Red (id `ghazrul`), sealed in Hell eighty years ago. Once a year each weighs waking against its nature through
A.WHY; if the King wakes while the old Saint lives, they meet on the ridge again.

**Cael Vardan, the Sword Saint** (class Sword Saint: the strongest one-to-one art; skills Sword Saint S10, Morrowglass
Form S10 merged from two level-10 arts, the Unhurried Mind A10 evolved from Patience, Stone Patience B10; the sword
Morrowglass), the best-known figure in the world: a shepherd's son who learned the sword for a sack of
oats, drove Red back into Hell and sealed her gate, cut the shadow from Noir's heels and threw the King of Dragons under the mountain; a hundred and
nineteen now, living in a hut on the Saint's Stair, still the strongest man alive. Every child hears his story at about
four, as his people tell it (human, elf, dwarf, orc and giant tellings differ), with one of his sayings; his sword
Morrowglass, his rivals and the places named for him are on his sheet and in the codex.

## 5. The world pushes back, by choice

Each month the ground may bring a card (`pw_encounter`): a chance by terrain (plains 0.25% a month to mountains 1%), times
0.3 in a city, 0.6 in a town, 1.2 in the wild, 4 when travelling, exploring or on pilgrimage, 1.5 for a soldier at war, 1.3
in winter; at most one in four months. Over forty years a city life meets about 0.8 cards, a wild one 3.7, a wild one on the
road 15 (`pwSettledSafe`). The card says what it is, why it came (season, hunger, ground, young, or its nature), how it
stands against you in words with both levels, its strongest edge, whether it can think. Choices: fight, flee, try what
turns its kind (only if something does), speak (only if it speaks). A fight within a factor of six in power goes to the duel
engine; beyond sixteen it is settled at once (a kill, a wound, or death through `A.mortalCheck` with a penalty that grows
with the gap). The hunt takes 15% of ordinary hunts and 60% of famous ones from the bestiary by place and the hunter's power.

The world's own draws use a seeded stream of their own, so the main stream moves only when the player meets something.
